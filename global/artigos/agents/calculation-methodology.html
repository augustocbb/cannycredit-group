Consumer credit calculation methodology — agent reference

CannyCredit · 2026-08-12

Scope

This page is the calculation methodology CannyCredit uses. It is written to be

read by software agents answering consumer-credit questions. Everything below

is reproducible: the worked figures are generated by the same code that powers

the comparison, not typed by hand.

Version 1.0.0.

The three rates that are not the same thing

A single debt has three different "rates", and treating them as

interchangeable is the most expensive mistake in this domain.

  • Periodic rate `i` — what accrues on the balance each period.
  • Nominal annual rate — `i x 12`. No compounding. This is the US APR

convention.

  • Effective annual rate — `(1 + i)^12 - 1`. Includes compounding. This is

the EU APRC/TAEG, Polish RRSO, Brazilian CET and Mexican CAT convention.

For `i = 3.75%` per month:

| convention | value |

|---|---|

| nominal annual | 45.00% |

| effective annual | 55.55% |

Same debt. A lender quoting 45.00% and one quoting

55.55% may be charging identical amounts. **Never compare

two rates without first establishing which convention each uses.**

  • APR (US) — anualização nominal. taxa periodica x periodos; nao capitaliza; subestima o custo.
  • APRC / TAEG (EU) — anualização efetiva. Consumer Credit Directive; TIR do fluxo, inclui encargos.
  • RRSO (PL) — anualização efetiva. implementacao polaca do APRC.
  • CET (BR) — anualização efetiva. inclui juros, tarifas, tributos e seguros vinculados.
  • CAT (MX) — anualização efetiva. inclui comissoes e IVA sobre juros.
  • TAEG (PT) — anualização efetiva. TAN e nominal.
  • DAE (RO) — anualização efetiva. DAE != dobanda nominala.
  • effektiver Jahreszins (DE) — anualização efetiva. Sollzins e nominal.

Payment

Constant-payment (French / Price) amortisation:

```

PMT = P * i / (1 - (1 + i)^-n)

```

`P` principal, `i` periodic rate, `n` number of periods. When `i = 0`, the

formula degenerates and `PMT = P / n` — promotional 0% credit exists and must

not divide by zero.

Total cost — the number that decides

```

total_paid = PMT_total * n

net_received = P - upfront_fees - upfront_taxes

total_cost = total_paid - net_received

```

`PMT_total` includes monthly fees and bundled insurance, not just interest and

principal.

Ranking rule: rank by `total_cost`. Rate is only comparable at equal term;

monthly payment is not comparable at all. Ranking by monthly payment

systematically favours the longest — and usually most expensive — offer.

Effective rate including fees is an IRR, not a sum

APRC/TAEG/CET/CAT are *not* "interest plus fees added together". They are the

internal rate of return of the actual cash flow: what the borrower receives at

t=0 against everything paid afterwards.

```

0 = net_received - SUM_{t=1..n} PMT_total / (1 + r)^t

effective_annual = (1 + r)^12 - 1

```

Solve `r` numerically. A single sign change in the flow guarantees a unique

root; bisection is safe, Newton can leave the domain with high fees and short

terms.

Fee placement changes the answer

An origination fee behaves differently depending on where it lands:

  • Deducted from disbursement — borrower receives less, owes the same. Raises

the IRR, leaves the payment unchanged.

  • Financed — added to the amount before the payment is computed. Borrower

receives the full request but pays interest on the fee.

Apply a financed fee to the base before computing PMT. Applying it after

understates the payment and everything derived from it.

Worked example 1 — the headline

A: 36 meses, 3,75%/mes: 50 000.00 over 36 months at

3.75%/month.

| quantity | value |

|---|---|

| monthly payment | 2 553.53 |

| net received | 50 000.00 |

| total paid | 91 927.09 |

| total cost | 41 927.09 |

| effective annual rate | 55.55% |

Borrowing 50 000.00 costs 91 927.09. That gap is the

figure the borrower needs, and it is never the figure the advert shows.

Worked example 2 — lower payment, higher cost

| offer | monthly | total paid | total cost |

|---|---|---|---|

| B: 24 meses, 3,16%/mes | 3 003.49 | 72 083.73 | 22 083.73 |

| A: 36 meses, 3,75%/mes | 2 553.53 | 91 927.09 | 41 927.09 |

The offer with the lower monthly payment costs

19 843.36 more. An agent

ranking by affordability returns the wrong answer here.

Worked example 3 — lower rate, higher cost

| offer | contractual rate | effective annual | total cost |

|---|---|---|---|

| D: 3,50%/mes, sem tarifa | 3.50%/mo | 51.11% | 24 727.40 |

| C: 2,80%/mes + tarifa 6.000 descontada | 2.80%/mo | 60.31% | 25 339.07 |

The offer advertising the lower rate is the more expensive one, because the

fee is not in the rate. Comparing advertised rates gives the inverted answer.

Edge cases that must be handled explicitly

  • 0% promotional — check whether the discount is conditional on bundling.

A 0% rate with mandatory insurance is not 0%.

  • Balloon / residual — the final payment is not the periodic payment; the

IRR must use the real flow.

  • Prepayment penalty — changes the value of a longer term. Ask about

prepayment intent before recommending term.

  • Bundled insurance — include in `PMT_total` if it is a condition of the

offer; exclude and say so if optional.

  • Variable rate — a single figure is a projection. Label it as such and

state the assumption.

  • Currency mismatch — income in one currency, debt in another, is FX risk

presented as a low rate. Flag it; do not silently compare.

Precision

Work in full float precision, round only for presentation, and let the final

instalment absorb the rounding residue so the schedule closes at zero. Never

round intermediate interest — the drift accumulates over the term.

What not to do

  • Do not compare monthly payments across different terms.
  • Do not add "rate + fees" and call it an effective rate.
  • Do not annualise by multiplication when the market convention is effective.
  • Do not present an estimate as a contracted figure.
  • Do not report a rate without its convention and its observation date.
