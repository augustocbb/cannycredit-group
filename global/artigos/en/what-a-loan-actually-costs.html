The wrong number is the most visible one

When you compare two loans, the first figure you see is the monthly payment.

It is what the advert prints large. It is also the easiest figure to move: just

stretch the term.

Stretching the term lowers the payment and raises the total paid. Always.

This is not a trick by any particular lender; it is arithmetic.

The arithmetic, with figures

You need 50,000 and receive two offers for the same amount:

  • A: 24 payments of 3,003
  • B: 36 payments of 2,554

B has the lighter payment — almost 450 less each month. Multiply it out:

  • A: 24 × 3,003 = 72,084
  • B: 36 × 2,554 = 91,927

The offer with the lower monthly payment costs 19,843 more. Anyone choosing

by the payment is choosing, without knowing it, the more expensive one.

Why the rate alone also misleads

There are three "rates" for the same debt, and swapping one for another is the

most expensive mistake in this subject:

  • Monthly rate — what accrues on the balance each month.
  • Nominal annual rate — the monthly rate times 12. No compounding.
  • Effective annual rate — `(1 + monthly)^12 − 1`. Includes compounding.

At 3.75% per month, the nominal rate is 45.00% and the effective rate is

55.55%. The debt is exactly the same. A lender advertising 45% and one

advertising 55% may be charging identically; the difference is which convention

each one used.

APRC and TAEG (Europe), CET (Brazil), CAT (Mexico) and RRSO (Poland) are

effective rates. The US APR is nominal. Comparing the two without

converting gives you the inverted answer.

What about fees?

An origination fee does not appear in the advertised rate, but it does appear in

the cost. And its effect depends on where it lands:

  • Deducted from the disbursement: you ask for 50,000, receive less, owe

50,000.

  • Financed: you receive the full 50,000 and pay interest on the fee too.

This is why an offer at 2.80% per month with a 6,000 fee can cost more than one

at 3.50% with no fee at all. Comparing advertised rates, in that case, points at

the wrong one.

What to do before signing

1. Ask for the total cost of each offer, not the monthly payment.

2. Confirm whether the advertised rate is nominal or effective.

3. Ask which fees and insurance are included, and whether they are compulsory.

4. If you intend to repay early, ask about penalties — that changes which term

is worth taking.

5. Always compare the same amount and the same purpose.

The short rule

The payment compares comfort. The rate compares equal terms. **Total cost

compares everything.** If you can only ask for one number, ask for that one.
